Abiogenesis Simulator

Can a planet build the chemical complexity required for life?

Abiogenesis Simulator is a computational framework that explores this question by simulating the progression of prebiotic chemistry under planetary conditions.

Given atmospheric composition, temperature, pressure, energy sources, solvent availability, and geological context, the simulator evaluates whether a planet can support the successive chemical stages believed to precede life.

Rather than asking "Is this planet habitable?", the project asks a more fundamental question:

Can this environment generate and sustain the chemistry necessary for abiogenesis?

The framework combines reaction-network expansion, molecular property analysis, and stage-wise chemical progression models to predict potential prebiotic pathways on both real and hypothetical worlds
Why this project exists

Most habitability assessments focus on liquid water, temperature, and location within a star's habitable zone.

However, life does not emerge directly from habitability.

Before cells, DNA, or metabolism can exist, a planet must first support a sequence of chemical transitions:

Formation of simple organic feedstocks
Synthesis of biological monomers
Polymerization into larger structures
Emergence of catalytic reaction networks
Development of self-sustaining chemical systems

A planet may be habitable while remaining chemically sterile.

Abiogenesis Simulator attempts to bridge this gap by evaluating prebiotic potential, not simply habitability.
What makes this different?

Most existing planetary habitability tools answer:

"Could liquid water exist here?"

This simulator instead asks:

"What chemistry can happen here?"

For a given environment, the system predicts:

Potentially formed molecules
Chemical progression bottlenecks
Viable prebiotic pathways
Stage-wise abiogenesis readiness
Overall prebiotic potential

The goal is not to predict life.

The goal is to evaluate whether the chemical prerequisites for life can plausibly emerge.
